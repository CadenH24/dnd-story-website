Collaboration and the power of D&D

With the sun coming to its precipice on a Sunday, in a small comic book shop Paradox Comics and Cards where a large group of Dungeons and Dragons (Also known as D&D) players gather to play in one fictional world but split between two tables.

Paradox holds a monthly D&D session for one big campaign for people who can’t come to paradox at their other set times.

As the Dungeon Masters (also known as DM) exit the staff only area from where they gathered supplies and prepared for the session.

“In most tabletop role-playing games (TTRPGs), one player takes on a facilitator role and guides the other players through a story. In D&D this is the dungeon master (DM)” said Orla Walsh in her paper on D&D and its impact of mental health.

The table is boisterous and rowdy as they talk about the events of their life from the past months with both confusion and laughter as they recall the last session and what happened.

As the recap begins the party of five players divert their attention to the DM to catch up from the previous session as they begin to recall events of what happened over two months ago for some players as they couldn’t make it to last session.

The session begins as the DM starts describing a town that the party has recently arrived at, with everyone looking with curiosity towards the DM, with the occasional joke being told, and notes hurriedly typed down.

When the DM finishes describing the town the players all start role-playing with each other and start asking the DM questions about what they can do in the town.

“The role of the player is to control their own individual character. In D&D, each player is invited to create a unique character,” said Walsh, “Players are also encouraged to write a character backstory and roll dice for their strengths and weaknesses (ability scores),”

As questions are asked and actions are completed the party is brought to a meeting with a bunch of delegates from across the world to discuss a growing issue within the world, a blight is infecting the world, and the party finds themselves asking the question how to handle the blight.The party concluded as in a previous session the party destroyed a great seal that is known to destroy the blight.

In D&D the DM also can voice Non-Playable Characters (NPC) to help the immersion of the game or to help the party.

As the meeting is adjourned the party is now chosen as the ones to destroy the rest of the seals around the world while running from the blight, it’s a race against the clock.

They bed down for the night and prepare to be transported to the capitol city to break one of the great seals. When they arrived at the capitol city, they would take that time to prepare.

The party decided to split up and get supplies for the upcoming trip. The Sorcerer (a magic caster born with magical abilities) goes into the shopping district to purchase supplies

While the Ranger (an archer who is attuned to the land and uses magic for utility) starts asking the locals for any information on the local government and what stories the public would know about the blight.

After some more preparations the party made their way to the sultan’s palace which was originally built to protect the seal from danger now used to house the sultan.

When the party entered and had an audience with the sultan, they were able to inform the sultan as to the dangers that were coming, went into the seal room which had pools recessed into the floor and vines hanging down from the ceiling however there was another figure in the room.

In a previous session one of the players was transported to a place between realms and was able to escape but unbeknownst to him a figure escaped with him and is now making their appearance using the seals magic properties to manifest within the world and is looking for a host.

With the figure lunging towards the party and mist rising from the ground forcing the players into the fight.

With the party jumping right into the fray the Ranger and Sorcerer try to bring down a pillar to crush the figure but comes to no avail while the Cleric (Someone blessed by a god with magic) dives into one of the pools and tries to hide in the water from the figure.

Later in the combat the Fighter (Well versed in melee fighting and using ranged weapons) charged straight towards the figure and tried to fight up close, while the Rouge (someone who hides from enemy’s to get an ambush and surprise them) went up behind the figure and tried to backstab them.

After a few rounds of combat, the Sorcerer sends a bolt of lightning from his hands that wrap around the figure and make the figure explode and sent the figure back to its original plane.

After the triumphant victory the party approached the sigil and discovered that thinking about destroying the rune would make the rune glow and when the other party members that had certain glyphs on their body from their earlier travels and the glyph would slowly start to dismantle itself revealing that the glyph is mechanical and the party caught a glimpse of the inner workings of the glyph and watched as it slowly disappeared completely unmade itself.

D&D has been used as storytelling device near its creation, but D&D has also been used to form collaborative efforts, and meet new people.

“Its just a fantastic experience to meet new people, tell new stories together and it’s just a lot of fun” said John Rodenbiker who is the director of cybersecurity operations for the state of North Dakota who’s been playing D&D off and on since the 1980s

D&D has been around for Decades but one thing is certain D&D couldn’t have happened without one man Gary Gygax,
