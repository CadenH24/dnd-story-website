Title

The sun sets on a Monday, and people start going to Paradox Comics-n-Cards to play Dungeons and Dragons to connect and relax. Where people from many walks of life come to play in a fantasy world. Dungeons and Dragons was established in 1974 under the company TSR (Tactile Studies Rules) with three men developing the entire game, Gary Gygax and David Arneson making three books the Players Handbook, Dungeon Masters Guide and a Monster Manual, only making 1,000 copies in Gygax’s basement. Gygax, born in Chicago Illinois, July 27, 1938, while growing up in Wisconsin he fell in love with war games like chess and pinochle, later in life he would play a tabletop war game called Gettysburg, but Gygax didn’t enjoy rolling the six-sided die and the bell curve that was associated, instead he put 20 poker chips In a bag numbered and asked his players to grab one poker chip out of the bag.

[add quote]

The new idea wouldn’t last long when he discovered the 20-sided die in a school closet, which was used back in the roman times for generating random numbers. In 1967 Gygax co-founded the International Federation of Wargamers (IFW), he took this idea to a rented out Horticultural Hall in Lake Geneva, Wisconsin and he would call it the Lake Geneva Wargames Convention, which would later be called Gen Con 0 where admission was , today Gen Con attracts over 70,000 attendees, in 1971 Gygax made his first game called Chainmail a table top strategy game that was focused on medieval fighting.

the next year at Gen Con 1 Gary Gygax met one man, David Arneson.

David Arneson was born October 1, 1947, in Hennepin County, Minnesota, when in high school Arneson played games in High School and joined the Midwest Military Simulation Association (MMSA), which were primarily high school and college age wargame fans. When Arneson went to college at the University of Minnesota, with a major in history and a love for Napoleonic- era naval battles Arneson made a game called Blackmoor that was inspiration of chainmail, he met Gygax at Gen Con 1 in August 1969 where Gygax fell in love with Arneson’s game.

Arneson and Gygax would soon make a game called Greyhawk a high-fantasy game with 50 pages of rules but the two realized while making it that Gygax is more focused on the rules while Arneson is more focused on freedom of the characters.

[add quote]

After sending the ruleset to play testers and receiving feedback they revised the ruleset to 150 pages and they renamed it to Dungeons and Dragons and made their own company in 1973 to publish it through Tactical Studies Rules (TSR), with financial help from Don Kaye. Don Kaye was Gygax’s childhood friend and helped with the publication of Dungeons and Dragons, Kaye passed away two years after TSR was made.

When Dungeons and Dragons was published it grew at a slow pace until the 1980s when popularity skyrocketed by word of mouth. It didn’t take long for the company to grow where eventually mainstream media got wind of it, but it wasn’t the attention they wanted.

[add quote from Shawn McMicheal]

On August 18, 1980, the New York Times would publish a story on James Dallas Egbert III who died of a self-inflicted gunshot wound, but the blame was directed towards Dungeons and Dragons due the reference of the game in his suicide note.

This event was one of many that would diminish the name of Dungeons and Dragons, in 1982 a movie starring Tom Hanks called Monster and Mazes would be released. Based off the book under the same name, the movie follows a college student that goes through psychosis and believes that he is in a table top role-playing game called Monster and Mazes and is stuck in this way of thinking for the rest of their life.

This was the start along with other forms of media and the news given false reports, the Satanic Panic was born.

[quote]

The Satanic Panic was a massive phenomenon that caused widespread panic about people worshipping Satan, Dungeons and dragons was one to receive the most backlash, Dungeons and dragons is played with one person as the Game Master who controls the table by guiding the players on adventures and telling the players how their actions affect a world made up by either the Game Master or a premade world by TSR.
