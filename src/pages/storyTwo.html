Title

The sun sets on a Monday, and people start going to Paradox Comics-n-Cards to play Dungeons and Dragons to connect and relax. Where people from many walks of life come to play in a fantasy world.

Dungeons and Dragons was established in 1974 under the company TSR (Tactile Studies Rules) with three men developing the entire game, Gary Gygax and David Arneson making three books the Players Handbook, Dungeon Masters Guide and a Monster Manual, only making 1,000 copies in Gygax’s basement.

Gygax, born in Chicago Illinois, July 27, 1938, while growing up in Wisconsin he fell in love with war games like chess and pinochle, later in life he would make a game called Chainmail but Gygax didn’t enjoy rolling the six-sided die and the bell curve that was associated, instead he put 20 poker chips In a bag numbered and asked his players to grab one poker chip out of the bag.

In 1967 Gygax co-founded the International Federation of Wargamers (IFW), he took this idea to a rented out Horticultural Hall in Lake Geneva, Wisconsin and he would call it the Lake Geneva Wargames Convention, which would later be called Gen Con 0 where admission was , today Gen Con attracts over 70,000 attendees, in 1971 Gygax made his first game called Chainmail a table top strategy game that was focused on medieval fighting.

The next year at Gen Con 1 Gary Gygax met one man, David Arneson.

“Dave was running a campaign using the Chainmailrules, a variant that sounded fascinating. So he and Dave Megary came down from the Twin Cities to my place in Lake Geneva to do some gaming,” said Gary Gygax in an interview with RPGnet.

David Arneson was born October 1, 1947, in Hennepin County, Minnesota, when in high school Arneson played games in High School and joined the Midwest Military Simulation Association (MMSA), which were primarily high school and college age wargame fans. When Arneson went to college at the University of Minnesota, with a major in history and a love for Napoleonic- era naval battles Arneson made a game called Blackmoor that was inspiration of chainmail, he met Gygax at Gen Con 1 in August 1969 where Gygax fell in love with Arneson’s game.

Arneson and Gygax would soon make a game called The Fantasy Game a high-fantasy game with 50 pages of rules but the two realized while making it that Gygax is more focused on the rules while Arneson is more focused on freedom of the characters.

“I was well aware of the need to use a working title, the need for some caution in regards using the actual name for a a projected game release. So that's the reason for that bland one on the draft works,” said Gygax

After sending the ruleset to play testers and receiving feedback they revised the ruleset to 150 pages and they renamed it to Dungeons and Dragons and made their own company in 1973 to publish it through Tactical Studies Rules (TSR), with financial help from Don Kaye.

Don Kaye was Gygax’s childhood friend and helped with the publication of Dungeons and Dragons, Kaye passed away two years after TSR was made.

When Dungeons and Dragons was published it grew at a slow pace until the 1980s when popularity skyrocketed by word of mouth. It didn’t take long for the company to grow where eventually mainstream media got wind of it, but it wasn’t the attention they wanted.

On August 18, 1980, the New York Times would publish a story on James Dallas Egbert III who died of a self-inflicted gunshot wound, but the blame was directed towards Dungeons and Dragons due the reference of the game in his suicide note.

This event was one of many that would diminish the name of Dungeons and Dragons, in 1982 a movie starring Tom Hanks called Monster and Mazes would be released. Based off the book under the same name, the movie follows a college student that goes through psychosis and believes that he is in a table top role-playing game called Monster and Mazes and is stuck in this way of thinking for the rest of their life.

This was the start along with other forms of media and the news given false reports, the Satanic Panic was born.

“My mom actually told me to tell people that you and I was accused of being a satan worshiper, that I should be like, no, actually, it’s the other way around. He asks to join our D&D group on Saturday nights but sometimes I tell him no,” said Shawn McMicheal a General Manager for Circle K and has been playing since 1st edition was released.

The Satanic Panic was a massive phenomenon that caused widespread panic about people worshipping Satan, Dungeons and dragons was one to receive the most backlash, Dungeons and dragons is played with one person as the Game Master who controls the table by guiding the players on adventures and telling the players how their actions affect a world made up by either the Game Master or a premade world by TSR.

After the panic was over around the 1990s, D&D fell into a rut and started back at ground zero, and fell out of the medias eyes until a small group of people started to live stream D&D for shows and podcasts.

The first group of people to do this was a group of voice actors popular within anime and video games, the group was called Critical Role.

“We believe that stories can change lives. There is something magical about escaping (even for just a little bit) the confines of our world in favor of what could be.” said Critical Role’s core value page.

After Critical Role more people wanted to play D&D and put their campaign on the web. Today we now have D&D campaigns across many media, mainly Video through streaming, and Audio through podcasts.

But through it all one thing has stayed the same, the love of the game, which can be shown with stadiums rented out and full for one D&D game or having one D&D group start a foundation and raise over a million dollars in their first year of starting the foundation.

“You can find a game that you can play with almost anybody,” said McMicheal
